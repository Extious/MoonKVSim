We replay native selector traces from NOSA, NSA, and MiniCPM using three PG19 documents per model, averaging two adjacent checkpoints within each document. All methods share the background order; Current, Recency, and the future-frequency reference differ only in their selected 4 MiB prepared residency. At 32 GB/s with zero compute, Current reduces raw-KV component time versus DRAM by 131.072, 131.072, and 128.683 microseconds, respectively, but matches Recency on the first two models and trails it by 2.389 microseconds on MiniCPM. At 256 microseconds assumed compute per layer, Current trails Recency by 209.436 and 218.124 microseconds on NOSA and NSA. Prepared residency therefore helps without establishing Current's superiority over Recency. This per-object transfer model assumes simultaneous query-demand release at layer entry, local tail availability, and prepared auxiliary state, excluding preparation cost. Object completion differs from the core simulator's atomic LoadBatch completion; the future-frequency reference is not a latency bound. The uncalibrated compute scan and full-prefill traces establish neither native-kernel recovery timing, chunked-resume equivalence, nor WCT gains.
