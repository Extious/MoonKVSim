\subsection{Adapter: Windowed Peer Capacity Control}
\label{sec:kv-runtime}
\label{sec:capacity-allocation}

The Adapter (4) controls the total Peer HBM budget $B_p$ on each
target DP. At a control-window boundary it publishes the next budget;
that target stays fixed within the window while every allocation checks
current physical space. KV Master owns backup decisions at the configured
checkpoint interval, request tiers,
and request-specific depths $L_r$. These depths can differ and change
at policy events inside the same capacity window.

\paragraph{Reserve memory for foreground inference.}
The next budget is the managed KV pool minus estimated foreground
KV demand and admission headroom, clamped between zero and half of
the pool. The pool excludes weights and fixed runtime state, but includes
existing Peer copies. Thus $B_p$ is a total target, not newly free space.
KV watermarks and memory-related admission stalls inform demand;
compute utilization alone does not imply insufficient KV capacity.
Increased foreground demand reduces the Peer target; sustained relief
permits expansion. The Adapter reports available, referenced, in-flight,
and reclaimable bytes so the Master can assess request-specific plans.
