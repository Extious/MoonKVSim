\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\system{} couples causal residual-workflow Slack to KV protection and recovery.
\textsc{SlackLandlord} manages continuous prefixes through independent HBM and
DRAM credits and pool-local victim frontiers; source-owned paths commit
independently, and \textsc{HBMQuotaAIMD} yields Peer HBM to foreground
pressure.  The same Slack orders committed-prefix loading and recomputation
under version and output fences.

Using frozen historical SGLSim data, an earlier AegisKV prototype reduces
mean WCT over no backup by 4.3\% in a paired DP8 trial, 11.8\% in a DP64 model
projection, and 18.9--30.4\% in a heavy-failure logical stress smoke.  The
paired trial also shows the prototype losing to simple host and peer-GPU
backup.  These results support the end-to-end value of avoiding KV
recomputation, but they do not validate the current design, isolate its three
algorithms, or establish physical serving performance.  Closing that gap
requires a current-design, multi-seed WCT campaign with executed failover and
contention-aware serving.  A separate frozen 3,110-run current-design
mechanism campaign provides complementary evidence: alpha decreases as
foreground pressure rises, reaches zero in sustained-pressure cells, and the
trace has 92.63\% request-level and 98.31\% token-level prefix reuse.  Its
occupancy, intended-residency, copy, and restored-token counters validate
simulator mechanisms and expose missing saturation, but remain distinct from
WCT and physical-system evidence.
